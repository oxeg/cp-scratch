# Lesson 2 - The shop: instructor plan

**New idea this week:** `if`, and Operators - comparing two numbers, and
arithmetic that feeds a variable back into itself.
**Floor (everyone must reach):** end of Milestone 3 - buy a Grandma with
cookies you clicked, and the counter climbs on its own afterwards. That is
an idle game, and it is the point of the whole course.
**Second shop item (everyone should reach):** Milestone 4 - the `Baker`,
built by duplicating `Grandma` and editing the copy in four places.
**Stretch (cosmetic tuning, optional):** which sprite the shopkeeper is and
her pose, her starting price, how steeply prices climb, what the Baker
earns, where the shop sits on the stage.

The game is playable after every milestone, so whatever the clock does,
nobody goes home with something broken. Milestone 2 is where the lesson can
be lost - budget it accordingly and read the section on explaining `not`
before the session, not during it.

## Timing

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00-0:08 | Recap last week, demo today's game |
| 0:08-0:14 | **M1 steps 1-7** Log in, open `Cookie Clicker`, check last week still works |
| 0:14-0:28 | **M1 steps 8-31** The shop opens - `Grandma`, `per second`, `grandma cost` |
| 0:28-0:36 | **M2 steps 1-11** Assembling `not <(cookies) < (grandma cost)>` - *the hard idea* |
| 0:36-0:52 | **M2 steps 12-32** What happens when she's bought - pay, count, double the price |
| 0:52-0:54 | The detour: you bought her, and nothing happened |
| 0:54-1:01 | **M3** Cookies bake themselves - *this is the floor* |
| 1:01-1:16 | **M4** The `Baker` *(second shop item - everyone should reach)* |
| 1:16-1:22 | Make it yours |
| 1:22-1:25 | Play each other's games |
| 1:25-1:30 | Save, confirm every project saved, wrap up |

Where those minutes come from. The step cards hold 101 numbered steps, and
this room is in its second week, so the nominal rate is 35 seconds a step -
faster than Lesson 1's 42, because nobody is meeting the editor for the
first time. Three rows are deliberately not at that rate - the opening row
and both Milestone 2 rows - and each is argued below rather than quietly
written into the table.

- **Recap and demo, 8 min.** No step content.
- **Milestone 1 steps 1-7, budgeted 6 min.** Seven steps at 35 seconds is
  4.1 minutes; the slot gets 6. **This slot is not free, and the next
  person to edit these cards needs to know that.** Logging in and finding
  last week's project are real numbered steps that cost real minutes, and
  the other course's Lesson 2 has a disclosed defect where exactly these
  four or five steps were added to an opening slot without the slot gaining
  a single minute. If you add steps here, add minutes here. The extra ~2
  minutes over the nominal rate are for the forgotten passwords and the
  kid who cannot find **My Stuff**.
- **Milestone 1 steps 8-31, 24 steps: 24 x 35s = 14 min.** Exactly 14.
- **Milestone 2, 32 steps at 45s = 24 min, split across two rows.** Not 35
  seconds a step. Nesting a `() < ()` inside a `not <>` inside an
  `if <> then`, and then dropping two ovals into the slots of the `<`, is
  the fiddliest block assembly in the whole course. Beginners miss the drop
  target, land the block next to the hole instead of in it, and have to try
  again. 45 seconds a step is the honest rate for this milestone and it is
  a quarter of the lesson. If you find yourself wanting the time back, take
  it from Milestone 4, not from here.

  The split costs nothing and buys you a checkpoint. Steps 1-11 build the
  condition and steps 12-32 build everything the `if` does when it fires,
  so **steps 1-11: 11 x 45s = 8.25 min, budgeted 8** and **steps 12-32:
  21 x 45s = 15.75 min, budgeted 16** - 8 + 16 = the same 24, with the
  0.25 minute moved from the first row to the second rather than lost.
  Announce the checkpoint: **everyone should have the condition assembled
  by 0:36.** A table that hasn't is a table to sit down with, and it is far
  better to know that at 0:36 than at 0:52. Do not push anyone past the
  condition to make the checkpoint - it is a signal to you, not a deadline
  for them.
- **The detour, 2 min.** Discussion time, not step time. See below.
- **Milestone 3, 12 steps: 12 x 35s = 7 min.** Exactly 7. This is the
  cheapest milestone in the course and it is the one that turns the game
  into an idle game - which is why the floor sits at the end of it.
- **Milestone 4, 26 steps: 26 x 35s = 15.2 min, budgeted 15.** Rounded
  down by 12 seconds. Milestone 4 sits above the floor and is the first
  thing cut, so it is the right row to absorb the rounding.
- **Make it yours, 6 min.** The only slot in the lesson with no fixed
  content to get through. A kid who runs out of ideas at minute 3 is
  finished, and a kid who hasn't can carry straight on tinkering while the
  room plays each other's games - so this is the row to raid if you need a
  minute back, and the only one that will not notice.
- **Play each other's games, 3 min.**
- **Save and wrap up, 5 min.** This is a floor, not a target. The save
  check below is a walk round every screen in the room, one at a time, and
  it will eat a combined "play and save" slot whole - leaving the playing
  to happen and the saving not to. It gets its own row for that reason.

Row by row, that is the whole 90: 8 recap + 6 opening + 14 M1 + 8 M2
condition + 16 M2 payload + 2 detour + 7 M3 + 15 M4 + 6 make it yours +
3 playing + 5 saving. Eleven rows.

Every minute in that list is either a step count times a rate (M1's two
rows, M2's two rows, M3, M4) or an argued fixed slot (recap, detour, make
it yours, playing, saving). The only number that is neither is the 1.9
minutes of slack in the opening row, and it is there on purpose, for
logins.

If you add or remove steps in the cards, redo this arithmetic and redo the
table - nothing checks it for you.

## What to demo

- **Recap, 2 minutes.** Open your own copy of last week's project, click
  the cookie, let them hear the sound and see the squash, press the green
  flag and show the counter drop to 0. Ask what the green flag block was
  for. Most of the room will remember, and the ones who don't get a free
  reminder of the pattern they are about to use four more times.
- **Today's game, the rest of the slot.** Show the finished version: click
  the cookie up to 50, buy the Grandma, and then **stop touching the
  mouse** while the counter keeps climbing. That silence is the sell. Then
  buy a second Grandma and let them see the price has doubled, and ask what
  they think happens if they buy ten.

Do **not** demo the shop while narrating how the `not` works. They meet
that in Milestone 2 and it lands better with a half-built script in front
of them than with your finished one on the projector.

## Blocks introduced today

- *Amber* Control: `if <> then` (new), `forever` (new),
  `wait () seconds` (they used this last week in the squash)
- *Green* Operators: `not <>` (new), `() < ()` (new), `() - ()` (new),
  `() * ()` (new) - the whole category is new this week
- *Orange* Variables: `set [variable] to ()` and
  `change [variable] by ()` from last week, now with three more variables
  in them, plus the `(cookies)`, `(per second)`, `(grandma cost)` and
  `(baker cost)` ovals dropped *into slots* rather than snapped under
  blocks
- *Yellow* Events: `when green flag clicked`, `when this sprite clicked` -
  both from last week, both reused today

The oval is the genuinely new *shape* this week, not just the new blocks.
Last week every block snapped underneath another block. This week ovals go
*into holes*. Say that out loud once, at the start of Milestone 2, and
point at the difference on a screen. Half the sticking points below are a
kid trying to snap an oval underneath something.

## How to explain `not <(cookies) < (grandma cost)>`

Do this at the whiteboard, to the whole room, at the start of Milestone 2.
It takes ninety seconds and a volunteer who fumbles it loses the milestone
for their table. The step cards already explain this to each kid in a short
paragraph, sitting between the eleven steps that build the condition and
the steps that build the rest, so you are not revealing anything - you are
drawing out on the board what they have already read on the card.

Say roughly this:

> "Grandma costs 50. When are you allowed to buy her? When you have 50 or
> more. So write me a block that says *cookies is 50 or more*."

Let them hunt the Operators palette. They will find `<` and `>` and `=`,
and they will not find "50 or more", because Scratch does not have a
"greater than or equal to" block. Let them fail to find it - that is the
whole beat. Then:

> "It isn't there. So say the opposite instead. *Not* allowed to buy means
> *cookies is less than 50* - and that block does exist. So: allowed to buy
> is **not** (cookies less than 50)."

Then write the three layers on the board in the order the cards build them,
outside in, and leave it up for the rest of the milestone:

```
if <                                 > then      <- the if
if <not <                 >>         then        <- the not, inside the if
if <not <(cookies) < (grandma cost)>> then       <- the < , inside the not
```

Two things to hold on to while circulating. First, kids will read `not` as
"doesn't" and get lost; say "flip it" instead - `not` takes true and gives
false, and takes false and gives true. Second, if a kid asks why they can't
just use `>` and lose the equals case, the honest answer is that they can,
and their game will refuse to sell a Grandma to someone with exactly 50
cookies. That is a good conversation to have and costs nothing; the card's
version is the one that behaves.

## The detour after Milestone 2 (0:52-0:54)

This is smaller than Lesson 1's green-flag detour but it works the same
way, and it is the reason Milestone 3 lands. Do not skip it because it is
only two minutes.

At the end of Milestone 2 a kid can click up 50 cookies and buy a Grandma.
`cookies` drops by 50, `per second` goes to 1, `grandma cost` goes to 100 -
and **nothing else happens at all.** The numbers moved. The game did not.
They have paid 50 cookies for a woman standing on the stage doing nothing.

Stop the room and sit in it for a moment. Ask:

> "You just paid 50 cookies. What did you actually get?"

Let someone say "nothing". Then:

> "Right - `per second` says 1, and nothing in your project has ever read
> that number. A variable is just a number written down. Something has to
> go and look at it."

Then start Milestone 3, which is twelve steps and is exactly the something.
The disappointment is what makes those twelve steps feel like a payoff
instead of a chore.

**Fast kids will already be in Milestone 3** when you call this, because
the cards are self-serve and that is what they are for. Don't drag them
back. Catch them individually afterwards with the same question - it works
just as well once their game is idling.

## Sticking points and fixes

- **An oval snapped underneath a block instead of dropped into a slot.**
  The single most common failure this week. Look for a stray `(cookies)`
  oval sitting loose in the code area. Fix: drag it into the hole itself
  and wait for the hole to light up before letting go.
- **The `not` missing, or the two ovals in `() < ()` the wrong way round.**
  Both produce the same symptom - clicking Grandma does nothing even with
  plenty of cookies - so check both. It must read
  `not <(cookies) < (grandma cost)>`.
- **Blocks snapped *under* the `if` instead of into its mouth.** Nothing is
  guarded any more, so cookies go negative and the price doubles on clicks
  that bought nothing. Very hard for a kid to see, because the script looks
  almost identical. Point at the C-shape: blocks inside are indented.
- **The three blocks inside the `if` in the wrong order.** If the
  price-doubling block runs before the paying block, the kid is charged the
  new price instead of the old one. Symptom: buying costs 100, not 50.
- **A variable made "for this sprite only".** `Grandma` reads `cookies` and
  writes `per second`. A variable made for one sprite only belongs to the
  sprite it was made on and **does not appear in any other sprite's
  palette at all** - so the kid is not silently misled, they are stopped
  dead: they get to Milestone 2 step 10, go looking for a `(cookies)` oval
  in `Grandma`'s Variables palette, and it simply isn't there. That "I
  can't find the block" is the report you will actually hear, and this is
  the first thing to check when you do. Fix: right-click the variable in
  the palette, delete it, make it again with **For all sprites** chosen,
  then repoint any dropdowns that lost their target.
- **A typo in `per second` or `grandma cost`.** Two-word names with a
  space, typed by twelve-year-olds. `persecond` and `per Second` are
  different variables as far as Scratch is concerned, and the script will
  look completely correct. Fix: right-click in the palette, **Rename**.
- **The two new `set` blocks landing in the click script in Milestone 1.**
  This has **no visible symptom** - `per second` is already 0 and
  `grandma cost` is already 50, so setting them again on every click looks
  exactly like not setting them. It only bites later, when a bought Grandma
  is wiped out by the next click of the cookie. The card makes it a
  deliberate count-the-blocks check, and it is worth your eyes too: four
  blocks under the green flag, six in the click script.
- **The Milestone 3 script snapped onto the end of the Milestone 1
  green-flag script.** Also **no visible symptom** - it genuinely works
  either way. Not worth chasing hard, but if you are looking over a
  shoulder, `Cookie` should have three scripts by the end of Milestone 3.
- **The `forever` block landing in the middle of the click script.** This
  one does have a symptom, and a confusing one: the counter climbs on its
  own, but the sound and the squash stop. **A `forever` is a cap block** -
  no bump on its bottom edge, nothing can attach below it - so dropping one
  into the middle of a stack knocks the blocks that were below it loose,
  and they end up sitting in the code area as a separate stack starting
  with `start sound`. **The fix has two halves and kids stop after the
  first one:** drag the `forever` out to an empty spot and give it its own
  `when green flag clicked` hat, *and then* snap the loose four-block stack
  back under `change [cookies v] by (1)`. A kid who only does the first
  half still has no sound and no squash, and will tell you the fix didn't
  work.
- **The `wait (1) seconds` block missing, or snapped above the `forever`.**
  Cookies race up about thirty times too fast. It cannot be *below* the
  `forever` - nothing can - so the place to look is between the `forever`
  and the `when green flag clicked` hat, where a `wait` waits one second
  once and then never again. **Easy to spot, awkward to fix** - do not tell
  them otherwise. The `wait` cannot simply be dragged down into the mouth:
  the `forever` is joined on below it and comes along with it, and that
  stack can never be dropped into the mouth of the `forever` it is
  carrying. A kid who tries the one-drag version ends up holding a stack
  nothing will accept, and usually drops it somewhere random. The card
  gives three drags in order - `forever` out to an empty spot (its `change`
  block riding inside the mouth), `wait` into that mouth above the `change`
  block, `forever` back under the hat. Read it with them rather than
  talking them through it from memory.
- **In Milestone 4, edits made on the wrong sprite.** `Grandma` and
  `Baker` have near-identical code areas. Have them click the sprite in the
  sprite list and read the name in the sprite info panel before touching a
  block, every single time.
- **In Milestone 4, one of the four `grandma cost` places missed.** The
  usual one missed is the oval inside the `() * ()` block at the bottom.
  Symptom: buying the Baker changes Grandma's price. Have them read the
  script out loud - there should be no `grandma cost` anywhere in it.
- **Testing Milestone 4 by clicking to 500.** Kids will try, and it eats
  the slot. Tell the room the shortcut the card gives: temporarily type
  `20` into `set [baker cost v] to (500)` on `Cookie`, test, put `500`
  back. Watch that they put it back.

## If the room is behind

Cut in this order:

1. **Milestone 4**, the `Baker`. It sits above the floor by design, and
   Lesson 3 works identically whether or not it exists - that is what the
   single `per second` variable is for. Nobody who stops after Milestone 3
   is missing anything next week.
2. **"Make it yours" at 1:16** - shorten it to two or three minutes, or
   read out one prompt instead of seven.
3. **The demo of today's game**, if you haven't given it yet - recap only,
   and let the cards carry the surprise.

**Milestones 1 to 3 are the floor and none of them can go.** Milestone 3 is
seven minutes and it is the one that makes the game an idle game; a kid who
goes home after Milestone 2 has paid 50 cookies for nothing and knows it.
If you are at 1:00 and the room is still in Milestone 2, cut Milestone 4
right then and announce it, rather than letting the fast table start it and
having to stop them.

## "Make it yours" prompts to read out

- "Who is your shopkeeper? Swap Grandma for a different sprite from the
  library, or give her a different pose by picking another costume."
- "What should she cost to start with? 50 was only a suggestion - try
  another number in `set [grandma cost v] to (50)`."
- "How steeply should prices climb? Change the `2` in
  `set [grandma cost v] to ((grandma cost) * (2))`. Try `3` for a brutal
  shop, or `1.5` for a gentle one."
- "What's your Baker worth? Try a different number in
  `change [per second v] by (10)`."
- "What does Grandma do when you buy her? Right now she takes your cookies
  in total silence - give her something to say with a *purple* Looks
  `say [] for () seconds` block, dropped into the mouth of the `if` on her
  script."
- "Who else could your shop sell? Add a third shopkeeper, built the same way
  the `Baker` was - a new sprite, a new cost variable, and the same script
  shape - and decide for yourself what he costs and what he earns."
- "Where does your shop live? Drag Grandma and the Baker around the stage,
  and drag the number readouts wherever you want them."

## Kids who finish early

Point them at `reference/extra-challenges.md` the moment their **Check it
works** passes while others are still building - not at the end of the
lesson. A kid with twenty spare minutes and nothing in front of them is the
one who starts poking at somebody else's keyboard.

**Match the tier to the clock.** The three-star challenge is a fifteen to
twenty-five minute build. Started at 1:10 it will not finish, and a kid who
leaves part-way through is worse off than one who never started. Big things
early in their spare time; the one-star tweaks near the end.

**On work you have not prepared.** The three-star challenges, and everything
in `reference/bonus-ideas.md`, are things nobody has built - including
whoever wrote them. When one does not work, the answer is "show me what you
tried", not you debugging something you have never seen. That is the honest
position at one volunteer per six kids, and saying it out loud costs you
nothing.

## Wrap-up and saving (1:25-1:30)

The step cards end with a numbered save step (**File -> Save now**, or just
**Save** in some versions). Don't assume it happened: **walk the room and
confirm every single project actually saved before anyone logs off.** Look
at each screen yourself. Lesson 3's step cards open by asking the kid to
open `Cookie Clicker` again, and a kid whose project didn't save has
nothing to open and cannot follow next week's cards at all. Two things go
wrong quietly - a kid who never reached the last card, and a "save" that
failed because the login had expired, which looks identical to a successful
one from across the room.

While you walk the room, check what next week opens on. Lesson 3 edits the
blocks built today, so don't let anyone finish with a variant of them:

- `Cookie`'s green-flag script sets `cookies` to 0, `per second` to 0 and
  `grandma cost` to 50 - Lesson 3 adds another `set` block to that same
  script, so it has to exist;
- `Cookie` has a second green-flag script with the `forever` loop in it -
  Lesson 3 edits the `change [cookies v] by (per second)` block inside it;
- a sprite named exactly `Grandma` with a working buy script;
- the variables named exactly `cookies`, `per second` and `grandma cost`,
  all lower case, all **For all sprites**;
- if they got to Milestone 4, a sprite named exactly `Baker` and a variable
  named exactly `baker cost`.

You do **not** need to note who missed Milestone 4. Lesson 3 asks nothing
about the `Baker` and works the same either way. Do note anyone who didn't
finish Milestone 3, because next week's very first script assumes the
project is already idling.
