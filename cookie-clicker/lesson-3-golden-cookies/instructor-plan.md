# Lesson 3 - Golden cookies: instructor plan

**New idea this week:** clones, and broadcasts.
**Floor (everyone must reach):** end of Milestone 2 - golden cookies that
appear at random, vanish on their own, and give a ten-second sugar rush when
you click one. That is today's game, and it is a visibly better game than
the one they took home last week.
**Further targets (for anyone who gets there):** Milestone 3, giving the
rush a sound and a signal you can see; Milestone 4, the "Cookie Master!"
message at a thousand cookies.
**Stretch (cosmetic tuning, optional):** how often golden cookies come, how
long they linger, how big the rush is, how long it lasts, what announces it,
what a golden cookie looks like.

This is the last lesson of the course. The game is playable after every
milestone, so whatever the clock does, everybody goes home with something
that works. Two things in this lesson need reading before the session rather
than during it: **the clone-click risk**, which can affect the whole room at
once and has a full fallback written out below, and **the hedge in Milestone
1**, where some kids have a `Special Cookie` from Lesson 1 and some do not.

Where those minutes come from. The step cards hold **169 numbered steps**,
and this room is in its third week, so the rate is **28 seconds a step** -
faster than Lesson 2's 35, because nothing about the editor is new to them
any more and the only genuinely new *shape* this week is the hexagon in
`wait until <>`. Every row below is either a step count times 28 seconds or
an argued fixed slot, and each one that is neither is named as such.

- **Recap and demo, 8 min.** No step content. Fixed slot.
- **Milestone 1 steps 1-17, 17 steps: 17 x 28s = 476s = 7.9 min, budgeted
  8.** These are the login steps *plus the hedge* - steps 8 to 17 build a
  `Special Cookie` for a kid who hasn't got one. **The slot is budgeted for
  the kids who run all seventeen, not for the kids who run seven.** A kid
  who already has a `Special Cookie` does steps 1-7 only, which is 3.3
  minutes, and then starts the next row early. That is fine and intended -
  do not hold them. If you add steps to this row, add minutes to this row.
- **Milestone 1 steps 18-62, 45 steps: 45 x 28s = 1260s = 21 min, plus 0.5
  min for the green-flag check, budgeted 22.** This is the biggest single row
  in the course and it is the week's hard idea, so it gets its honest number.
  Ten of those 45 steps are two `pick random` blocks being filled in four
  slots each, which is dull but fast; the eight paint-editor steps at the
  start are the ones that will drift, and the "that's gold enough" cue below
  is how you stop them drifting. The **0.5 min** is not step time: after step
  41 the card stops the kid with an un-numbered check - press the green flag
  and watch the small gold cookie disappear and stay gone. That lands about
  41 steps and 27 minutes into the lesson, and it is the first time anything
  the kid has built today does something they can see. Without it the room
  goes 62 steps and 29 minutes from opening the project to the first golden
  cookie, which is the longest unrewarded stretch in the course and it sits
  on the week's hard idea. Do not let anybody skip it, and do not treat its
  half-minute as free. The remaining **0.5 min** of the row is buffer against
  the paint-editor drift.
- **Milestone 2 steps 1-11, 11 steps: 11 x 28s = 308s = 5.1 min, plus 0.8 min
  for the whole-room clone check, budgeted 6.** The 0.8 is not step time: the
  card stops the kid at step 11 and tells them to press the green flag, wait
  for a golden cookie and click it. Waiting for a spawn can take half a
  minute on its own, and this is the checkpoint where you find out whether
  the room needs the fallback. See **The clone-click risk** below.
- **Milestone 2 steps 12-51, 40 steps: 40 x 28s = 1120s = 18.7 min, budgeted
  18.** This row is **deliberately budgeted 40 seconds under its nominal
  pace**, and that 40 seconds is most of what pays for the two un-numbered
  checks above. It is the right row to take it from: it is the second half of
  a milestone the room is already inside, and it is straight block-dragging
  with no new idea in it. Across both rows Milestone 2 gets 24 minutes
  against 23.8 minutes of step time plus a 0.8-minute check, so it is running
  about 40 seconds tight. Watch the clock on it.
- **Milestones 3 and 4 share one 15-minute slot**, and this is the only row
  in the table that is not sized to finish what it contains. The honest
  arithmetic:
  - **Milestone 3 is 30 numbered steps, but no kid runs all 30.** Steps 1-12
    are the sound, and then the card offers a choice: steps 13-17 are a
    speech bubble, steps 18-30 are a second backdrop. The speech-bubble
    route is 17 steps = **7.9 min**. The backdrop route is 25 steps =
    **11.7 min**.
  - **Milestone 4 is 26 steps = 12.1 min.**
  - Fifteen minutes buys Milestone 3 by either route, and 7 minutes of
    Milestone 4 by the fast route or 3 by the slow one. **Most of the room
    will not finish Milestone 4**, and the table is not pretending
    otherwise. Both milestones are named "further target" on the card and in
    the header above, and the 0:05 announcement tells the room the same
    thing before anybody starts. A kid who stops halfway through Milestone 4
    has a working game; the two new scripts are additions to `Cookie`, not
    edits of anything that already runs.
- **Make it yours, 5 min.** Fixed slot, and the one to raid if you need a
  minute back - a kid who runs out of ideas at minute 3 is finished, and a
  kid who hasn't can carry on tinkering while the room plays each other's
  games.
- **Play each other's games, 3 min.** Fixed slot. It is the last lesson, so
  do not skip it.
- **Save and wrap up, 5 min.** Fixed slot, and a floor rather than a target.
  The save check is a walk round every screen in the room, one at a time,
  and it will eat a combined "play and save" slot whole - leaving the
  playing to happen and the saving not to. It gets its own row for that
  reason.

Row by row, that is the whole 90: 8 recap + 8 M1 opening and hedge + 22 M1
build + 6 M2 clone script and check + 18 M2 sugar rush + 15 M3 and M4 + 5
make it yours + 3 playing + 5 saving. Nine rows.

The four step-derived rows are budgeted at 8 + 22 + 6 + 18 = **54 minutes**
against **52.7 minutes** of measured step time (113 steps x 28s = 3164s).
That leaves **1.3 minutes** across those four rows for content that is not
numbered steps, and there are exactly two such things: the green-flag check
after Milestone 1 step 41 (**0.5 min**) and the whole-room clone check after
Milestone 2 step 11 (**0.8 min**). 0.5 + 0.8 = 1.3. Row by row the slack is
+0.07 (opening), +0.5 (M1 build, after the check is paid for), +0.07 (M2
first row, after the check is paid for) and **-0.67** (M2 second row, run
deliberately fast) - which nets to -0.03, so the four rows are subscribed to
within two seconds of their budget. The five fixed slots are 8 + 15 + 5 + 3 +
5 = 36. 54 + 36 = 90.

If you add or remove steps in the cards, redo this arithmetic and redo the
table - nothing checks it for you.

## What to demo

- **Recap, 2 minutes.** Open your own copy of last week's project. Click the
  cookie, buy a Grandma, take your hands off the mouse and let them watch
  the counter climb. Ask what `per second` is for. That is the whole of last
  week in ninety seconds.
- **Today's game, 3 minutes.** Show the finished version. Press the green
  flag, click the big cookie, and then **wait, saying nothing**, until a
  golden cookie pops up somewhere unexpected. Click it, and let them watch
  the counter accelerate for ten seconds and then settle back down. Do it
  twice, so they see it appears somewhere different the second time. Then
  ask: "how many golden cookie sprites do you think are in my sprite list?"
  The answer is one, and that is the week's idea.
- **Then say what today actually delivers.** This is the 0:05 announcement
  and it matters more than the demo. Say something close to:

  > "Today's game is the golden cookie. That's Milestone 1 and Milestone 2,
  > and everybody is going to get there. After that there are two extra
  > things on the cards - making the rush loud, and a Cookie Master message
  > at a thousand cookies. They're extras. Most of you won't get to the
  > second one, and that is completely fine: the golden cookie is the
  > lesson."

  Say it before anybody opens their project. Milestones 3 and 4 sit in one
  15-minute slot that cannot hold both, and a room that was promised four
  milestones will finish the session feeling like it failed at a game that
  actually works.

Do **not** demo the clone fallback, and do not mention `touching` or
`mouse down?` at all unless you need them. If the room needs them you will
find out at 0:44 and can teach it then.

## Blocks introduced today

- *Amber* Control: `create clone of [myself]` (new),
  `when I start as a clone` (new), `delete this clone` (new),
  `wait until <>` (new, Milestone 4), plus `forever` and `wait () seconds`
  from last week
- *Yellow* Events: `broadcast [message]` (new), `when I receive [message]`
  (new), plus `when green flag clicked` and `when this sprite clicked` from
  weeks one and two
- *Purple* Looks: `hide` (new), `show` (new), `say [] for () seconds` (new),
  `switch backdrop to [backdrop]` (new, Milestone 3), plus `set size to () %`
  from week one
- *Blue* Motion: `go to x: () y: ()` (new) - the first Motion block in the
  whole course
- *Green* Operators: `pick random () to ()` (new), `() > ()` (new,
  Milestone 4), plus `() * ()` from last week
- *Orange* Variables: `set [variable] to ()` and the `(bonus)`,
  `(per second)` and `(cookies)` ovals, all from earlier weeks
- *Pink* Sound: `start sound [sound]` from week one, reused in Milestone 3
  on a second sound the kid adds to `Cookie`
- *Pale blue* Sensing: `touching [mouse-pointer]?` and `mouse down?` -
  **only if the clone-click fallback is needed.** They are in the cheat
  sheet for that reason and nowhere else.

Two ideas, not just two piles of blocks. Say each of them out loud once.

**A clone is a copy the game makes for itself while it is running.** It is
not in the sprite list, you cannot click on it in the editor, and it has its
own position and its own size. The real `Special Cookie` hides at the green
flag and is never seen again all game; every golden cookie a kid sees is a
clone of it. Say that at the start of Milestone 1, because the `hide` block
in step 29 looks like a mistake until you have.

**A broadcast is a shout.** One sprite shouts, and every sprite and every
clone hears it at the same moment. It is the answer to a question they have
already run into twice: how does one sprite make something happen in
another? Say that at the start of Milestone 2. `Special Cookie` shouts
`sugar rush` and then deletes itself - it is gone before `Cookie` has
finished reacting, and the shout still lands. That surprises kids and is
worth pointing out.

## The hedge in Milestone 1: who needs a `Special Cookie`

Lesson 1's Milestone 4 sat above that lesson's floor, so some of this room
has a `Special Cookie` and some does not. It is the only place in the whole
course where the cards hedge on what a kid built in an earlier week, and it
is handled inside eight minutes.

**Spot the second group in one pass, before the room starts building.** At
about 0:09, once everybody is logged in and looking at their project, walk
the room and read sprite lists. You are looking for the name
`Special Cookie`. That is faster than asking - kids do not reliably remember
what they built two weeks ago, and several will say yes because they
remember a small cookie and mean the one they never finished.

**Tell the two groups apart out loud, once.** Something like: "if you can
see `Special Cookie` in your sprite list, skip steps 8 to 17 - they're not
for you. If you can't, do them; they take two minutes and then you're with
everybody else." The card says the same thing in a box between step 7 and
step 8, so you are repeating it, not revealing it.

**Do not stall the first group while the second group catches up.** The kids
who already have a `Special Cookie` should go straight on to step 18 and
start recolouring. The whole point of putting the hedge in numbered steps
rather than in a "wait, everybody" announcement is that the room never has
to move at one speed.

Three things to watch while the second group duplicates:

- **`Cookie` now has three scripts, so the copy arrives with three.** Steps
  15 and 16 delete two of them - the setting-up green-flag script and the
  green-flag script with the `forever` in it. **Both have to go.** If the
  `forever` one is left behind, the kid quietly gets double the per-second
  income all game, and it looks like a working game, just a strangely
  generous one. Lesson 1's version of these steps only deleted one script,
  because in week one there was only one to delete; do not go from memory.
- **A kid who did not finish Lesson 2's Milestone 3** has no `forever`
  script to copy, so they will find only one green-flag script to delete.
  The card covers this in its `Stuck?` box - do step 15, skip step 16 - but
  the bigger problem is that the same kid has nothing for Milestone 2 steps
  45 to 51 to edit either. Catch that now, not at 0:55. Twelve steps of
  Lesson 2's Milestone 3 will fix it and is worth doing at their elbow.
- **Size 50, not a redrawn cookie.** The Size field is the right tool here
  because there are no `set size to` blocks left on this sprite once step 17
  has run.

## The clone-click risk, and the whole-room fallback

**Read this section before the session.** It is the one thing in this course
that can stop the whole room at once.

Milestone 2 assumes that a *clone* responds to `when this sprite clicked`.
Nobody has verified that in the version of Scratch your club actually runs.
It is believed to work, and this course does not assert Scratch behaviour
nobody has checked, so the fallback is written out here, in full, and in the
card's `Stuck?` box as well.

**Verify it yourself before the session if you possibly can.** Five minutes
in the editor answers it: make a sprite, give it
`when green flag clicked / create clone of [myself v]` and
`when this sprite clicked / say [yes] for (2) seconds`, press the flag and
click the clone. If it says yes, none of the rest of this section matters.

**How to recognise in the room that everybody needs the fallback, inside a
minute.** The card builds `Special Cookie`'s new script in eleven steps and
then stops the kid with a deliberate mid-milestone test: press the green
flag, wait for a golden cookie, click it, and it should vanish. Nothing else
should happen yet - nothing is listening to the broadcast - but it should
vanish. That test lands at about 0:44 for most of the room, and it is why
that row carries 0.8 minutes it did not earn from its step count.

At 0:44, **stop the room and take a show of hands - but ask two questions,
not one.** Do not debug anybody until you have counted both.

> **First:** "hands up if you've finished step 11 - the golden cookie's new
> script is built and you've pressed the green flag."
>
> **Then, to those hands only:** "keep your hand up if you clicked a golden
> cookie and watched it disappear."

**Two questions, because one is confoundable and this is the single
mechanism the lesson's biggest risk rests on.** This room is deliberately
desynchronised: the kids who already had a `Special Cookie` skipped ten
steps at 0:09 and are running about five minutes ahead, and the kids who
built one have no slack at all. A single question - "did your golden cookie
vanish?" - counts everybody who has not got to step 11 yet as a failure, so a
room that is merely *behind* reads as a room that needs the fallback. That
mistake costs four minutes of whole-room teaching plus a Sensing detour
nobody needed, on the tightest row in the table.

Read the second count as a fraction of the first:

- **Nearly all of the first group's hands stay up.** It works. The few that
  drop are ordinary mistakes - wrong sprite, wrong dropdown on the
  `broadcast` block, or they clicked the small gold sprite that is now hidden
  rather than a clone. Deal with them individually and carry on.
- **Nearly all of them drop.** It is not their mistake, it is the editor.
  Stop the room - the whole room, including the kids who have not reached
  step 11 - say so plainly ("this is Scratch, not you") and teach the
  fallback to everybody at once, on the projector. It costs about four
  minutes of Milestone 2's second row and it is recoverable; four minutes
  spent going desk to desk finding out the same thing eleven times over is
  not.
- **Very few hands went up to the first question at all.** You have learnt
  nothing about clones, only that the room is behind. Do not teach the
  fallback. Circulate, get people to step 11, and re-ask both questions three
  or four minutes later.

**The fallback, in full.** Delete the `when this sprite clicked` script the
kid has just built (drag it onto the palette). In its place, on
`Special Cookie`, build a **second** `when I start as a clone` script:

```
when I start as a clone
forever
  if <<touching (mouse-pointer v)?> and <mouse down?>> then
    broadcast [sugar rush v]
    delete this clone
```

Building order on the projector: `when I start as a clone` hat into an empty
spot; `forever` snapped under it; `if <> then` into the mouth of the
`forever`; an `and <>` block from Operators into the six-sided hole in the
`if`; `touching [mouse-pointer v]?` from Sensing into the left half of the
`and` and `mouse down?` into the right half; then
`broadcast [sugar rush v]` into the mouth of the `if`, and
`delete this clone` snapped under it, still inside the mouth.

Four things to say while you build it:

1. **The other clone script stays exactly where it is.** A sprite is allowed
   two `when I start as a clone` scripts and both run for every clone. The
   `go to` / `show` / `wait (5) seconds` / `delete this clone` script is not
   being replaced. Kids will delete it if you do not say this.
2. **Nothing goes below the `forever`.** It is a cap block - no bump on its
   bottom edge - so the `if` goes *inside* its mouth and that is the end of
   the script.
3. **`touching (mouse-pointer v)?` alone is not enough**, and neither is
   `mouse down?` alone. The first fires when the mouse merely passes over
   the cookie; the second fires when the mouse is clicked anywhere on the
   stage. You need both at once, which is what `and` is for.
4. **Everything after this is identical.** The broadcast is the same
   broadcast, `Cookie`'s receiver is the same receiver, and Milestones 3 and
   4 do not change at all. Nobody is behind.

## Sticking points and fixes

- **A kid with no `Special Cookie`, spotted late.** See the hedge section
  above. The cost of finding this at 0:20 instead of 0:09 is about six
  minutes of that kid's Milestone 1.
- **The copied `forever` script left on `Special Cookie`.** Only affects
  kids who ran steps 8 to 17. The symptom is a game that idles at double
  speed, which looks like a working game. If you see a kid whose counter is
  climbing suspiciously fast with their hands off, click `Special Cookie`
  and count its scripts - after step 17 it should have exactly one.
- **The `hide` in the wrong place.** Two different problems that sound the
  same. If `hide` is *missing* from the green-flag script, the real
  `Special Cookie` sits visibly on the stage all game and kids click it
  instead of the clones. **The green-flag check after step 41 catches that
  one for you**, which is most of why it is there - the kid finds it
  themselves, at the moment they built it, instead of you finding it at 0:40.
  If `hide` is inside the `forever`'s mouth rather
  than above it, **there is no symptom at all** - the game behaves
  identically, because what you see on the stage is a clone and the clone
  shows itself. The card makes that second one a deliberate look-at-the-
  script check rather than pretending it can be played.
- **The `wait` block above the `forever` instead of in its mouth.** The loop
  runs flat out and the stage fills with golden cookies within a second of
  the green flag. It cannot be *below* the `forever` - nothing can - so the
  only place to look is between the `forever` and the `hide` above it, where
  a `wait` waits once and then never again. Dramatic, obvious, easy to fix.
- **`delete this clone` missing from the bottom of the clone script.**
  Golden cookies pile up and stay. Scratch stops making new clones once
  there are enough of them, so the eventual symptom is a stage covered in
  frozen gold cookies and no new ones arriving. This is the habit to name
  out loud: **every clone script ends by deleting the clone.**
- **A `pick random` dropped beside a slot rather than into it.** The oval
  sits loose in the code area and the `go to x: () y: ()` block keeps the
  number that was already typed there, so every golden cookie appears in the
  same place or the same vertical line. Same fix as last week's ovals: drag
  it right onto the hole and wait for the hole to light up.
- **The paint bucket filling a chocolate chip instead of the cookie.**
  Ctrl+Z, then click a part of the big circle with no chip on it. Also worth
  a "that's gold enough" cue around 0:22, the same way Lesson 1 cues "that's
  good enough" during the painting - a kid can spend the whole 21-minute row
  on the Brightness slider.
- **The clone does not respond to being clicked.** See the whole section
  above. Do not debug this one kid at a time.
- **The second `set [bonus v] to (1)` left saying `0`.** A `set` block
  arrives from the palette saying `0`, and card step 42 is the one that
  changes it. Get this wrong and the first golden cookie of the game kills
  the game: `bonus` becomes 0 ten seconds later, clicks are worth 0 and
  `(per second) * (bonus)` is 0. The symptom - "my game stopped counting" -
  arrives ten seconds after a moment of triumph, so kids do not connect the
  two. First thing to check when you hear it.
- **The `wait (10) seconds` missing from the receiver.** The two `set`
  blocks run back to back and `bonus` is never observably 2. Reported as
  "nothing happens when I click a golden cookie", which is also the
  clone-click symptom and also the wrong-message symptom - so look at the
  script, do not diagnose from the description.
- **`broadcast` and `when I receive` pointing at different messages.** Kids
  who mistype `sugar rush` at the **New message** box and then correct
  themselves end up with two messages in the list, one letter apart.
  Symptom: the golden cookie vanishes when clicked and nothing else happens.
  Fix: open both dropdowns and pick the same entry in both.
- **`set [bonus v] to (1)` landing in `Cookie`'s click script.** `Cookie`
  has three scripts by now and two of them start with a green flag, so card
  step 17 spends a line saying which. If it lands in the click script,
  `bonus` is reset to 1 on every click of the big cookie and a rush ends the
  instant the kid does the one thing they always do during a rush.
- **The `() * ()` block with an empty slot.** Scratch reads an empty slot as
  0, so the idle income stops completely, rush or no rush. The most common
  version is dragging the `() * ()` block straight onto the
  `(per second)` oval, which pops the oval out loose and leaves both slots
  empty. The card avoids that by taking the oval out first, in step 46, but
  kids who work ahead will hit it.
- **A variable made "for this sprite only".** `bonus` is made on `Cookie`
  and only ever used on `Cookie`, so this one does not break the game the
  way it would have last week - but the stage readout says `Cookie: bonus`
  instead of `bonus`, and the challenge sheets assume it is shared. Fix:
  right-click it in the palette, delete it, make it again with **For all
  sprites**, and repoint the three `set` dropdowns and the two ovals.
- **The `say` block above `set [bonus v] to (2)` in Milestone 3.**
  `say [] for () seconds` holds up the script it is in, so the doubling
  waits two seconds for the bubble to finish. Symptom: the announcement
  arrives before the rush does. It belongs under `start sound`.
- **The stage stuck on the rush backdrop.** Pressing the green flag during a
  rush stops the receiver where it stands, so the switch-back never runs.
  Not a bug in their script. Fix: Stage panel, Backdrops tab, click the
  backdrop they want.
- **`(cookies) > (999)` built the wrong way round in Milestone 4.** Reads as
  "999 is bigger than my cookies", which is true at the green flag, so the
  message fires immediately and then never again. The other failure -
  `9999` typed for `999` - looks identical to the script never working at
  all. Both are in the card's `Stuck?` box with the shortcut for testing:
  temporarily put `9` in place of `999`.

## If the room is behind

Cut in this order:

1. **Milestone 4**, "Cookie Master!". It is the last thing built, it is
   two standalone scripts, and nothing depends on it. If the room is still
   in Milestone 2 at 1:05, announce that Milestone 4 is off the table rather
   than letting the fast table start it and having to stop them.
2. **Milestone 3**, making the rush obvious. Cut the backdrop route first
   and leave the speech bubble - steps 1 to 17 are eight minutes and still
   give the rush a sound and something to see.
3. **"Make it yours" at 1:17** - shorten it to two or three minutes, or read
   out one prompt instead of six.

**Milestones 1 and 2 are the floor and neither can go.** Milestone 2 is what
makes the golden cookie worth clicking; a kid who goes home after Milestone
1 has a decoration, not a game. If you are at 1:00 and the room is still
mid-Milestone 2, take the whole 15-minute further-targets slot and spend it
finishing Milestone 2 with them. That is a good outcome, not a failure - it
is the outcome the 0:05 announcement promised.

## "Make it yours" prompts to read out

- "How often should golden cookies come? Try different numbers in
  `pick random (15) to (30)`. `5` to `10` makes the game frantic; `60` to
  `120` makes a golden cookie a real event."
- "How long should one linger before it vanishes? That's the `5` in the
  clone script's `wait (5) seconds`. Try `2` if you want it to be hard to
  catch."
- "How big should the rush be? `2` was only a suggestion - try `3` in
  `set [bonus v] to (2)`. Leave the other one at `1`, because 1 is the
  number that means no rush."
- "How long should a rush last? Try a different number in
  `wait (10) seconds` inside the `when I receive [sugar rush v]` script."
- "What announces a rush? A different sound, different words in the bubble,
  a different backdrop - or all three at once."
- "What does a golden cookie look like? Open `Special Cookie`'s Costumes tab
  and draw on it: a star, a crown, a shine."

Two of those prompts change a number that a kid has just spent twenty
minutes getting right, so say once, out loud, that changing `bonus` to 3 is
allowed and changing the second `set [bonus v] to (1)` is not. Everything
else on the list is safe to break and easy to put back.

## Wrap-up and saving (1:25-1:30)

The step cards end with a numbered save step (**File -> Save now**, or just
**Save** in some versions). Don't assume it happened: **walk the room and
confirm every single project actually saved before anyone logs off.** Look
at each screen yourself.

This is the last lesson, so the usual reason for insisting - next week's
cards open this project - does not apply, and that makes it more important
rather than less. This save is the one the kid keeps. Two things go wrong
quietly: a kid who never reached the last card, and a "save" that failed
because the login had expired, which looks identical to a successful one
from across the room.

While you walk the room, three things are worth doing on the last day:

- **Get the project shared if your club does that**, so kids can open their
  game at home and show somebody. That is the whole point of three weeks of
  work and it takes one click on **Share**.
- **Note anyone whose game is broken rather than unfinished.** Unfinished is
  fine and expected - three quarters of the room will have no Cookie Master
  message and that is what the plan predicted. Broken is different: a game
  where clicking does nothing, or where the counter stopped after the first
  golden cookie, is nearly always the `set [bonus v] to (0)` above, and it
  is a fifteen-second fix worth making before they close the tab.
- **Say what they built.** Three weeks ago they had an empty project. They
  now have a game with a shop, an economy, prices that rise, an idle income
  and a random bonus event. Kids do not notice that on their own.
